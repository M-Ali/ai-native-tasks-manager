I scanned all seven brands on Instagram and looked at the actual artwork — 84 posts (12 per brand, the logged-out ceiling), collected 27 August 2026, coded on the nine-dimension schema. Everything below comes off the creative, not the captions.

**One caveat first:** this is organic, owned-channel output only. It says nothing about spend, reach or media weight, and because the sample is capped at 12 per brand I can't and won't give you a share of voice.

## What the category is actually doing

**Nobody proves anything.** 68 of 84 posts (81%) carry no proof device at all. Eight cite a number, and three of those eight are Meezan quoting an award it gave itself airtime for. In a category whose entire product is trust, the proof column is empty.

**The banks have become a discount feed.** Brand-building as a share of output: Bank Alfalah 8%, Meezan 0%, UBL 0%. Alfalah ran two separate eight-column instalment price tables (TCL LEDs, American General appliances, 3–24 months) as social tiles — unreadable at feed size. Its most human line, "Forgot to pay your Bill AGAIN?", is buried in a service post. And one Alfalah post is a video whose entire frame is the logo on white.

**UBL has handed its brand to the State Bank.** Seven of twelve posts carry the Raast lockup, usually bigger than UBL's own mark — "Only with Raast, the Best Payment Network for Digital Transactions." 75% of its posts drive to an app. The one exception is the biggest number in the whole scan — "Commitment of 40 billion Rupees", PKR 10bn for a new university, 22bn Khushhali Bank equity, 8bn agriculture subsidy — and it never repeats it.

**Meezan is publishing its trophy cabinet.** "SIX-TIME BEST BANK 2018, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025 & 2026" ran three times in the window, plus a ribbon-cutting with executives in frame. The largest Islamic bank in the country led on faith-based value exactly once in twelve posts. Its own strongest territory is standing empty.

**HBL is the only bank still trying to mean something** — six of twelve posts are brand-building — but in three unconnected directions: Hajj (Urdu-first, badged Exclusive Hajj Banking Partner with the Ministry of Religious Affairs), She's Next with Visa, and management-trainee recruitment. Volume without a spine.

**The smallest bank is running the only campaign.** JS Bank, at 35.9K followers versus HBL's 137K, carries six of twelve posts on "Zindagi Karo Roshan" — JS Solar Finance for clinics, schools and homes. "PATIENTS KA BHAROSA BARQARAR — apnay clinic ko rakhein powered bina interruption ka JS Solar Finance kay saath." Eligibility criteria published openly in Roman Urdu. The night-lit clinic and the family in a lit room are the only commissioned documentary photography in all 84 posts. (It undercuts itself by casting a male padel player on the JS Her women's debit card.)

**The two fintechs own the voice, on a fraction of the volume.** Easypaisa is phone-native, nine of twelve posts cast GenZ, and it names costs no bank will touch — "har ATM apna", "All funds received in easypaisa are free-of-charge and will remain free-of-charge." It even sells a term deposit as a meme: "gold star behaviour: auto-investing with term deposit instead of impulse-buying." SadaPay publishes its own performance numbers — "50.4% New Customers Onboarded", "Built on trust. Grown by you." Correcting for pinned posts, the four banks are publishing at roughly 10–30 posts a week; Easypaisa and SadaPay at 1–2. Volume is not the battleground.

## The gap

**The vacant territory is proof — and the customer nobody is speaking to is over 44.**

Four things support it, all countable from the scan:

1. **81% of category output proves nothing.** One credible, repeatable, publishable number would stand alone in this category. SadaPay has already shown it works and is the smallest advertiser here — which means the position is claimed, not held.
2. **60% of posts contain no person at all.** Where there is one, the casting reads young: 33 of the 51 codeable posts are aimed at under-43s (inference from casting, styling and language, not a fact about who banks). Exactly one post in 84 addresses the over-60s — Meezan's Senior Citizen Account. The segment with the deposits is being served price tables.
3. **Fraud is the loudest anxiety in the category and nobody converts it.** Eight posts touch scams; all are one-way warnings. Fear appears three times in 84 posts. Only one post in the entire scan gives the customer actual control — UBL's Urdu "apna account fori freeze karein". A bank that made fraud protection its proposition rather than its public-service duty would be alone there.
4. **Savings and returns are near-absent.** Two posts in 84. Only Meezan puts a rate on the artwork (10% p.a. on COII). In an economy where rate is the reason to move money, the category is advertising 5% cashback on an electricity bill.

Where I'd take a retail bank: stop competing on the discount tile, which everyone wins and nobody keeps, and build the campaign on a number the client can publish every quarter and competitors structurally can't copy — complaints resolved, fraud reversed, time to open, disbursements made. Then cast it for the customer with the balance, not the customer with the phone.
