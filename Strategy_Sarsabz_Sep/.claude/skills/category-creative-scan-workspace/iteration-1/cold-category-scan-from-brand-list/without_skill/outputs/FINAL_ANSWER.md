## Follower base (Aug 2026)

UBL 196K · Meezan 163K · Easypaisa 155K · HBL 137K · Bank Alfalah 106K · SadaPay 48.7K · Alfa 42.1K · JS Bank 35.9K

Note the inversion: Easypaisa outranks HBL and Bank Alfalah on a fraction of the budget.

## What the category is actually posting

**66% tactical, 34% brand.** Offer graphics, discount offers, rate offers and contests are 39% of all output on their own. The modal tone is "functional" (28 of 67 items). This is a category running a merchant-discount calendar and calling it social.

Brand by brand:

- **HBL** — refreshed its identity in Feb 2026, but the feed is a product catalogue: Hajj banking, business debit card, appliance financing, car loans, iPhone financing. Brand layer is institutional (She's Next with Visa, #LeadTheNew trainee content, Euromoney awards). Telling detail: HBL's own published social case study is about **TikTok**, not Instagram.
- **UBL** — biggest following, thinnest creative. A corporate PKR 40bn nation-building commitment, repeated Raast payment education, "Tap. Pay. Win" with Google Wallet, and single-outlet merchant discounts (15% at one restaurant in Bahawalpur).
- **Meezan** — the sharpest product marketer here. Rate-led (CoI ~10%), award-led ("Best Bank, 6th time"), and the only bank credibly addressing two growth segments: students (Student Debit Card) and freelancers (Payoneer integration).
- **Bank Alfalah** — highest cadence, multiple posts a day, all card-led: 0% markup installments, appliance plans, Platinum lounge access, auto-debit how-tos. One brand asset ("The Way Forward", 8 Aug). Its Alfa account is a pure commerce feed — AlfaMall, Vivo pre-orders, J. Fashion, BNPL on Apple — plus a heavy Roshan Digital diaspora block.
- **JS Bank** — smallest following, most distinct positioning. **JS Her** is the category's only real segment brand, with genuine product behind it (Pakistan's first maternity insurance via JS Her; Khud Mukhtar entrepreneur financing PKR 100k–5m). Feed is community-first: women's netball, Green Day '26, campus activations, padel, Basant. Only brand tilting brand-over-tactical.
- **Easypaisa** — the only brand in the category with a voice. Bio reads "like for the ease stay for the memes 💚"; a recent caption is literally "I am tired of making content. Can you all just order debit cards?" Backed by real work with BBDO — "Easy Wala Bank" for the digital-bank transition, and an anti-advertising campaign that serves people cat reels instead of ads. Even fraud warnings are memes.
- **SadaPay** — "Money Made Simple", 4m+ signups, Papara-owned since 2024. Sharp identity, but the anti-bank challenger energy has softened into travel bookings, giveaways and contests. Its actual differentiator, SadaBiz for Upwork/Fiverr freelancers, is barely marketed.

## Where the gap is

**1. Nobody is talking about money — they're talking about discounts.** No bank on Pakistani Instagram is doing financial confidence, money anxiety, or the universal stuff: first salary, first car, supporting parents, saving for a wedding, inflation. Easypaisa touches it only through jokes.

**2. Humour is a monopoly of one — and it's a fintech.** Four humour posts in 67, all Easypaisa. Every incumbent bank is 100% straight-faced. That caution isn't paying off: the meme account has more followers than HBL.

**3. Islamic banking is a compliance label, not a creative territory.** Meezan owns the largest Islamic franchise in the country and posts about deposit rates and awards. Nobody has built emotional creative around riba-free living or halal earning — the single most differentiated proposition in this market, sitting unused.

**4. Women are a card, not an audience.** JS Her is the only women's platform and its Instagram expression is salon and play-area discounts, while category-first product (maternity insurance, entrepreneur financing) goes unsold. HBL's She's Next is one post. In a market with one of the world's widest gender gaps in account ownership, this is the biggest unclaimed territory here.

**5. The freelancer economy is served by product, not brand.** Meezan has Payoneer, SadaPay has SadaBiz. Neither has built a campaign around it. Large, young, digitally native, high deposit velocity — and no bank speaking its language.

**6. Everyone posts; nobody has a series.** Not one brand runs a recurring named format, character, or serialised slot. Every feed is a stream of one-offs. Cheapest structural advantage available and it's vacant.

**7. Fraud education is a duty post.** Three brands do it; only Easypaisa makes it watchable. Scam awareness is the one banking topic Pakistanis actively share, and it's being published as a grey warning graphic.

## The angle I'd take into the pitch

Build the retail brand on **financial confidence, delivered in a recurring format, in a real voice** — and pick one under-served audience (women or freelancers) to own outright rather than adding another discount to the pile. The two things that actually build brands on Instagram — personality and serialisation — are completely unoccupied in this category, and the only player exploiting either is a fintech, not a bank. That's the argument, and the client's competitors have handed it to us.
