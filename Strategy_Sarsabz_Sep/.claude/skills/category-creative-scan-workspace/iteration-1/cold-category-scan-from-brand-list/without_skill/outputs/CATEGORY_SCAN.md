# Pakistani retail banking — Instagram category scan
Scan date: 27 August 2026

## Method and its limits (read this before quoting anything)

- Source: live Instagram profile pages for each brand, fetched and read directly, plus trade/press searches to identify named campaigns.
- Coverage: 67 distinct recent posts/themes surfaced across 8 accounts (7 brands; Bank Alfalah counted twice because its app brand **Alfa** runs a separate feed).
- **No engagement data.** Instagram does not expose likes/comments/views to an unauthenticated fetch. Nothing here claims what performed — only what is being *published*. Share-of-voice and engagement benchmarking would need a paid listening tool (Meltwater/Brandwatch/Socialinsider) or the Meta Ad Library for paid.
- **No exact posting-frequency counts.** Bank Alfalah's feed was the only one that surfaced dated items (5 posts across 25–27 Aug alone). Treat cadence statements as directional.
- Handles verified: @hblpak, @ubldigital, @meezanbanklimited, @bank_alfalah, @alfabybankalfalah, @jsbankltd, @easypaisa, @sadapay.

## What the coding says

67 items coded on post type, brand-vs-tactical intent, audience and tone.

- **66% tactical, 34% brand.** Two-thirds of the category's Instagram output is an offer, a discount, a rate or a contest.
- **Post-type concentration:** offer graphic (13) + discount offer (7) + rate offer (2) + contest (4) = 26 of 67 items, i.e. **39% is a price/perk unit**.
- **Tone:** functional (28) dominates everything. Humour appears 4 times — all Easypaisa. Aspirational appears 3 times. Purpose appears 4 times (HBL She's Next, UBL nation-building, JS Bank netball and Green Day).
- **Audience:** "mass"/"mass adult" is 22 of 67. Only three brands segment meaningfully — JS Bank (women), Meezan (students, freelancers), Easypaisa/SadaPay (Gen Z, freelancers).

## Brand by brand

**HBL (137K).** Refreshed its brand identity in Feb 2026 and is rolling it out across touchpoints. Feed is a product catalogue: Hajj/Islamic banking, business debit card, summer appliance financing, car loans, iPhone financing with Mercantile. Brand layer is thin and mostly institutional — She's Next with Visa (women entrepreneurs), #LeadTheNew management-trainee content, award wins (Euromoney 2026 Best Bank + Best Retail Bank). Notable: HBL's own case-study material is about **TikTok**, not Instagram. Instagram reads like an obligation channel.

**UBL (196K).** Biggest following, thinnest content. Two poles: a corporate PKR 40bn nation-building commitment, and Raast/instant-payments education repeated across several posts. Plus "Tap. Pay. Win" (Google Wallet, monthly smartwatch) and single-outlet merchant discounts (a 15% deal at one Bahawalpur restaurant). Largest audience in the category being fed the least distinctive creative.

**Meezan (163K).** Most disciplined product marketer in the set. Rate-led (Certificate of Investment ~10%), award-led ("Best Bank in Pakistan, 6th time"), service-led (24/7 centre at Clifton Bridge), and it is the only bank credibly speaking to two specific growth segments — **students** (Student Debit Card, padel discounts) and **freelancers** (Payoneer integration). Zero faith-based emotional storytelling despite owning Islamic banking outright; the Shariah proposition is a category label, not a creative territory.

**Bank Alfalah (106K).** Highest observable cadence — multiple posts a day, all card-led: 0% markup installments, appliance plans, Visa Platinum lounge access, supplementary cards, auto-debit how-tos. One brand asset ("The Way Forward", 8 Aug). Alfa, the app brand, is a **pure commerce feed** — AlfaMall, Vivo pre-orders, J. Fashion, BNPL on Apple, Imtiaz Raast QR — plus a heavy Roshan Digital diaspora block (Apna Ghar, Apni Car at KIBOR+1%). Alfalah has effectively split "bank" and "shop" into two accounts and let the shop win.

**JS Bank (35.9K).** Smallest following, most distinct positioning. **JS Her** is the only genuine segment brand in the category — a women's card with real product behind it (Pakistan's first maternity insurance through JS Her, JS Khud Mukhtar entrepreneur financing PKR 100k–5m). The feed is culture-and-community first: women's netball, Green Day '26, campus (LUMS, Procom, Banca), Matchpoint Padel, Basant. It is the only brand whose ratio tilts brand-over-tactical. Weakness: the JS Her posts that do appear are mostly salon and play-area discounts — the product story is far stronger than the creative expression of it.

**Easypaisa (155K).** The only brand in the category with a **voice**. Bio: "like for the ease stay for the memes 💚". Captions like "I am tired of making content. Can you all just order debit cards?" Backed by real campaign work with BBDO — "Easy Wala Bank" for the digital-bank transition, and an anti-advertising campaign that gives people cat reels instead of ads. Products (savings pockets, auto-invest, term deposits) are marketed as consumer utility, not banking. Fraud awareness delivered as memes. This is the benchmark the pitch has to answer.

**SadaPay (48.7K).** "Money Made Simple", 4m+ signups, Papara-owned since 2024. Feed is travel bookings (flights, hotels), card lines ("the card built to go as far as you do"), permanent contests, fraud awareness and team BTS. Sharp visual identity, but the anti-bank challenger energy has softened into travel offers and giveaways since acquisition. Its real differentiator — SadaBiz for Upwork/Fiverr freelancers — is under-marketed.

## Where the gaps are

**1. Nobody is talking about money. They are talking about discounts.**
39% of the category's output is a price or perk unit; the modal tone is functional. The entire category has outsourced brand-building to a merchant-discount calendar that no one screenshots. There is no bank on Pakistani Instagram doing financial confidence, money anxiety, or the genuinely universal stuff — first salary, first car, rent vs. buy, supporting parents, saving for a wedding, inflation. Easypaisa touches it only through jokes.

**2. Humour is a monopoly of one, and it's a fintech.**
Four humour posts in 67 and all of them are Easypaisa. Every incumbent bank in the set is 100% straight-faced. That isn't caution paying off — it's a bank with 155K followers and a meme voice outperforming banks with 10x the marketing spend.

**3. Islamic banking is a compliance label, not a creative territory.**
Meezan owns the largest Islamic franchise in the country and posts about deposit rates and awards. Nobody has built emotional creative around riba-free living, halal earning, or the values conversation — despite it being the single most differentiated proposition available in this market.

**4. Women are a card, not an audience.**
JS Her is the only women's platform, and its Instagram expression is salon and play-area discounts. There is genuinely category-first product underneath (maternity insurance, entrepreneur financing) that is being marketed like a loyalty programme. HBL's She's Next is one post. In a market with one of the world's widest gender gaps in account ownership, this is the largest unclaimed territory in the set.

**5. The freelancer economy is being served by product, not by brand.**
Meezan has Payoneer. SadaPay has SadaBiz. Neither has built a campaign around Pakistan's freelance workforce — a large, young, digitally native, high-deposit-velocity segment that currently has no bank speaking its language.

**6. Everyone posts. Nobody has a series.**
Not one brand in the scan runs a recognisable recurring format — no named weekly slot, no ongoing character, no serialised content anyone would follow for its own sake. Every feed is a stream of one-offs. That is the cheapest structural advantage available and it's sitting there.

**7. Fraud education is treated as a duty post.**
Three brands do fraud awareness (Meezan, Alfalah, SadaPay) and only Easypaisa makes it watchable. Scam awareness is the one banking topic Pakistani audiences actively share. It is being published as a grey warning graphic.

## Recommended angle for the pitch

Build the retail brand on **financial confidence, in a recurring format, with a real voice** — and pick one under-served audience (women or freelancers) to own outright rather than adding another discount to the pile. The category has left the two things that actually build brands on Instagram — personality and serialisation — completely unoccupied, and the only brand exploiting either is a fintech, not a bank.
