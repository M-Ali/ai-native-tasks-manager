Why these six

No brand set was given, so this is the US mass and dermatological skincare shelf — the set that competes for the same drugstore basket and the same skincare feed. Two derm-authority incumbents (CeraVe, Cetaphil), one pharmacy-prestige player (La Roche-Posay), one ingredient-transparency disruptor (The Ordinary), and two barrier-era challengers built on social from day one (BYOMA, Bubble). They span the generational range you asked about — Bubble sells to Gen Alpha, La Roche-Posay to ageing Gen X — so the gaps between them are readable rather than cosmetic.

What is evidence here, and what is judgement

  • Observed: live profile pulls on 27 Aug 2026 — bios, follower counts, post dates, media type, and Meta’s auto alt-text. Cadence and format skew are counted from those tiles.
  • Observed: named 2025–26 trade-press and brand-owned campaign coverage, cited under each brand.
  • Judgement: the generation read and the brand-build/tactical split. Instagram does not expose full caption text on the profile page, so post-by-post caption coding was not possible. Each call is shown with the evidence it rests on — treat the meters as a defensible read, not a measurement.
  • Cadence is a floor. The feed view truncates at about 12 tiles, so real posting volume is equal to or higher than shown.

What the pattern says

Nobody is discounting in feed

Across 66 tiles in the window, not one visible price or discount post. The only retail mechanic anywhere in the set is La Roche-Posay’s Ulta gift-with-purchase — and that lives in a press release, not the grid. Caveat: captions were not retrievable, so this is an absence of visible evidence rather than proof.

Derm authority has stopped being a differentiator

Four of six bios lead on dermatologists — including both challengers. When Bubble, built for twelve-year-olds, and Cetaphil, built for sensitive adults, make the same claim, the claim is table stakes and the fight moves to tone.

The Gen X flank is nearly empty

Only La Roche-Posay speaks to over-45s, and only through one franchise. Five brands are crowded onto Gen Z. Mature and menopausal skin is the vacant territory in this set.

Cadence and fame trade off

The Ordinary posts least (~3.5/wk) and has the largest following (3.0M); Cetaphil posts most (~8/wk) on the smallest (222K). Volume is compensating for a brand idea, not creating one.

Two grammars of proof

Incumbents prove with credentials and celebrity; challengers prove with routines and peers — carousels, five-step explainers, ambassador content. Format follows argument: video for fame, carousel for teaching.

Both challengers are ageing up at once

BYOMA’s clinical 2.0 era and Bubble’s cross-generational push are the same manoeuvre in the same year. They are about to collide in the millennial middle that CeraVe already owns.

What would sharpen this

Three gaps are worth closing before this goes in front of a client, and all three are collection problems rather than analysis ones.

  • Caption-level coding. Full caption text is the evidence that would turn the brand/tactical meters from a defensible read into a count. It needs the Instagram Graph API, a licensed listening tool, or manual capture of roughly 50 posts per brand.
  • TikTok, where the category actually lives. Every brand here is described in trade coverage as TikTok-led, and none of that output is in this scan.
  • Paid versus organic. The Meta Ad Library would separate what each brand pays to say from what it posts for free — the fastest way to see where tactical spend really sits.
