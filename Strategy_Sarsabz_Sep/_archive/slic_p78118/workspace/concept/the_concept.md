# Campaign Concept

State Life Insurance Corporation of Pakistan · EPADS P78118 · Campaign Concept (15 marks)
Draft 1, 2026-08-28.

Every claim below traces to the 288-advertisement audit (`workspace/audit/deep/capture.xlsx`),
to State Life's own published creative, or to a cited third party. Nothing is asserted
without a source.

---

## A note on the heritage figure

State Life was established under the **Life Insurance (Nationalisation) Order of 19 March
1972**, with the corporation taking over consolidated management on **1 November 1972**.
That is **54 years** in 2026, and it is what State Life's own advertising says — *"Securing
Pakistanis' Future Since 1972"* and *"For over 50 years"*.

If a longer claim is wanted, there is a legitimate one available but it must be worded
carefully: State Life was formed by merging **32 existing life insurance companies**, several
of which predate Partition. A line such as *"the promises we inherited go back further than
Pakistan"* is defensible; *"60 years of State Life"* is not, and a competitor or an evaluator
could check it in a minute. **Confirm the anchor date before any line goes to artwork.**

## The stated assumption

The tender's **Section VII, Schedule of Requirements, is referenced at ITA 6.8 but is absent
from the issued document**, and the clarification window has closed. No product line,
audience or objective has been specified.

**We have therefore built the concept at corporate brand level**, on the reasoning that a
brand platform can carry any product line beneath it, whereas a product-specific concept
cannot be widened. Where a product focus is required, the platform's first executions point
to individual life and retirement — the two lines where the audit shows the category's
attention is weakest.

This assumption is stated here deliberately rather than left implicit.

## Part 1 — Four directions, ranked

Each is a genuine, ownable route. They are ranked by how they perform against the tests in
Part 2, not by preference.

### Direction A — *The Promise You Can Check* — **RANK 1, RECOMMENDED**

**Territory:** Proof. Trust as a published, verifiable number rather than a stated feeling.

**Execution format:** **Reason-why**, supported by **documentary testimonial**. Reason-why
advertising leads with the substantiation rather than the benefit — it is the correct format
when the category's problem is disbelief rather than ignorance, and it is the format the
category has almost entirely abandoned.

**What the audit supports it with:**

- **233 of 288 category advertisements (81%) carry no proof device at all.** No statistic, no
  rating, no testimonial, no guarantee.
- **Three advertisements in 288 cite a performance number. Two of them are State Life's** —
  the E-Kachehri posts of 13 August (745 complaints received, 744 resolved) and 22 July
  (730 received, 729 resolved).
- **State Life already runs a testimonial series**, *Policyholder's Voices*, with named
  claimants — Sara Rizvi (Islamabad), Mahin Khan (Karachi).
- Only **14 of 288 advertisements (5%)** address the category's core objection at all.

**Why it is defensible:** E-Kachehri is a **statutory grievance-redress obligation**, not a
marketing programme. It generates the resolution figure every cycle whether or not marketing
wants it. A private competitor can publish an annual claims total; it cannot manufacture a
recurring public complaints-resolution mechanism, because it does not have the duty that
creates one.

**Risk:** the number must keep being good. This is addressed in Part 4.

---

### Direction B — *Aye Khuda Meray Abbu Salamat Rahain* (revival) — **RANK 2**

**Territory:** Intergenerational love, expressed as a prayer rather than a purchase.

**Execution format:** **Emotional drama**, in the **slice-of-life** tradition — everyday
domestic moments carrying the weight, rather than staged jeopardy. Correct for a line whose
power is recognition, not persuasion.

**What the audit supports it with:**

- State Life revived this line for Father's Day 2026. Its own caption reads: *"For over five
  decades, 'Aye Khuda Meray Abu Salamat Rahain' has echoed through Pakistan."*
- It is **the only line in the entire 288-advertisement sample with multi-decade cultural
  equity.** Askari's *Iraada Karo* dates from 2025; Jubilee's *Befiker Mustaqbil* is
  recent; nothing else comes close.
- **Two advertisements in 288 are cast for anyone over 60, and both are State Life's** — the
  generation for whom this line is a memory, not a slogan.

**Why it is defensible:** fifty years of equity cannot be bought, borrowed or out-spent.
Reviving it is also a cheaper and more flattering recommendation than replacing it.

**Why it ranks second, not first:** it is a *feeling*, not a *proof*. It does not answer
*will they actually pay* — the objection the audit shows is unoccupied. On its own it risks
nostalgia without a reason to act. It is strongest as the **emotional layer of Direction A**,
which is what Part 3 recommends.

---

### Direction C — *Trusted by Your Parents. There for Your Children.* — **RANK 3**

**Territory:** Generational continuity — the institution that outlasts the individual.

**Execution format:** **Two-generation vignettes**, a slice-of-life structure showing the same
family relationship across time.

**What the audit supports it with:** this is State Life's own existing execution, carrying
*"Securing Pakistanis' Future Since 1972"*. It is already produced and already on air.

**Why it ranks third:** heritage is the *second-most-crowded* territory in the category.
`national_trust` appears in **29 of 288** advertisements — but **22 of those 29 sit in the
`pride` register**: flags, cakes, Independence Day, a fighter jet. Only seven give a reason
to buy anything. Adamjee is running *"70 years"* and *"A Decade of Dedication"*; EFU is
running *"Born from a DREAM"*. Continuity is true for State Life and ownable in degree, but
it is a claim about the **past**, and the category is already noisy there.

---

### Direction D — *Claim What Is Yours* — **RANK 4**

**Territory:** The unaddressed policyholder — matured policies, dormant benefits, the over-60s.

**Execution format:** **Problem–solution**, executed as **direct response**. A different job
from a brand platform: it seeks an action, not a belief.

**What the audit supports it with:**

- State Life already runs this: *"Claim your matured policies and secure your benefits now!"*
- **Two advertisements in 288 are cast for the over-60s. Both are State Life's.** The segment
  with the assets, the dependants and the nearest claim is addressed by nobody else in the
  category.
- The audit's `product_focus` shows the category funnelling output into rewards apps —
  a mechanic that structurally cannot serve this audience.

**Why it ranks fourth:** it is a **segment campaign, not a brand platform**. It cannot carry
Communication Strategy, Creative Artworks and Media Mix beneath it, which is what the other
55 marks require. It is, however, an excellent **first burst inside Direction A** — a
demonstration that the promise is real, aimed at people owed money today.

## Part 2 — How the four score against the tests

| Test | A · Proof | B · Aye Khuda | C · Continuity | D · Claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Swap test** — could a competitor run it tomorrow? | No — needs the statutory mechanism | No — needs 50 years | **Partly** — Adamjee and EFU already claim heritage | No — needs the policy book |
| **Asset test** — structural, not tonal? | Sovereign backing + statutory duty | 50-year cultural equity | Heritage + scale | 55% share, largest book |
| **Deletion test** — does the audit make it inevitable? | Yes — 81% prove nothing | Partly — audit shows equity, not need | Weak — heritage is crowded | Yes — 2 ads in 288 |
| **Department test** — who must change? | Claims and grievance must hold the number | Nobody | Nobody | Operations must handle the volume |
| **Month-twelve test** — does it generate work? | Yes — the figure refreshes each cycle | Needs a mechanic adding | Limited — a claim, not an engine | No — a burst |
| **Feeds the other 55 marks?** | Yes | Yes | Partly | No |

## Part 3 — The recommendation

**Run Direction A as the platform, with Direction B as its line.** Direction D becomes the
first burst; Direction C is absorbed as a support point rather than run separately.

This pairing is the recommendation because it solves each direction's weakness with the
other's strength: **A has the argument but no warmth; B has the warmth but no argument.**

### The chain

| Step | |
|---|---|
| **Asset** | State ownership: the promise is underwritten by the state, and a statutory grievance obligation generates a published resolution figure every cycle. 55% market share (CCP, 2025). A 54-year line already in the culture. |
| **Tension** | *Will they actually pay?* Unanswered — 81% of category advertising proves nothing, and only 5% addresses the objection at all. |
| **Territory** | **Proof.** Trust as an audited, publishable, repeatable number — not trust as a feeling. |
| **Proposition** | *State Life publishes what it settles — because a promise you can check is the only kind worth making.* |
| **Line** | **Aye Khuda Meray Abbu Salamat Rahain** — the prayer stays; the campaign answers it. |
| **Mechanic** | The **resolution figure, published every cycle.** It refreshes itself, so the platform generates work rather than repeating a launch. |

### Why the line and the proposition are different things

The proposition is the argument the brand makes. The line is what the work carries. Here the
relationship is unusually strong: the line is a **prayer**, and the platform's job is to
answer it. *You have prayed for him for fifty years. We are the ones who keep the promise
when the prayer is not enough.*

That relationship — a question the audience already says aloud, and an institution that
answers it with a number — is the concept.

## Part 4 — What it asks of State Life

Stated plainly, because the price of an idea should be visible before it is bought.

**1. The figure must keep being published, and it must keep being good.**
A published resolution rate is only proof while it holds. A bad quarter becomes public too.
This is a genuine commitment and it is **State Life's decision, not the agency's** — we
present it as a choice:

- **Commit to the cadence.** The figure becomes the campaign's spine and the category's
  benchmark. Nobody else can follow, because nobody else has the obligation that produces it.
- **Claim the territory without the cadence.** Safer, materially weaker, and copyable within
  a quarter by any competitor willing to publish first.

**2. Claims and grievance must be inside the campaign, not downstream of it.**
The department test: this idea is only true if the operation performs. It should not launch
without those teams in the room.

**3. Confirm the heritage anchor** — 1972, or a defensible predecessor claim. Before artwork.

## Part 5 — How it travels

Enough to prove the platform works, in the channels the tender gates on. Full executions
belong to the Creative Artworks submission; this is the demonstration that the idea carries.

**By channel** — the tender gates on **APNS, PBA and PID accreditation** and asks for
published print advertisements and TVC/DVC as evidence of campaign experience. The platform
is therefore built press- and broadcast-first:

| Channel | Format | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Press | Reason-why, full page | The figure, published. Print is where a number is read as a record. |
| Television | Emotional drama, 60" and 30" | The prayer. The line does the feeling; the endline does the proof. |
| Radio | Testimonial | *Policyholder's Voices* in the claimant's own voice — the format costs least and travels furthest in Urdu. |
| Outdoor | Mnemonic | The figure alone, at scale. |
| Digital | Documentary, direct response | Long-form claimant stories; Direction D's claim-your-policy burst. |

**By audience** — the platform's first extension is the **over-60s**, unaddressed by every
competitor in the sample, and the audience for whom the prayer is a memory rather than a
line.

**By language** — **204 of 208 captioned advertisements in the category are Latin-script
only; four carry any Urdu.** A platform whose line is already Urdu should be executed
Urdu-first. In a category that has reached 3% of the population while advertising in English,
this is a territory in itself and costs nothing to take.

**By time** — month one is the launch film and the first published figure. Month twelve is
the fourth figure, the fourth set of claimant stories, and a benchmark the category is now
measured against.
