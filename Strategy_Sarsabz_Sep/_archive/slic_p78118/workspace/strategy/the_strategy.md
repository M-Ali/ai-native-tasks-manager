# Communication Strategy

State Life Insurance Corporation of Pakistan · EPADS P78118 · Communication Strategy (15 marks)
Draft 1, 2026-08-28.

Answers the five parts the criterion lists, in its order: **communication objective, target
audience, communication approach, messaging direction, communication journey.**

Every figure traces to the 288-advertisement audit (`workspace/audit/deep/capture.xlsx`), to
State Life's own published creative, or to a cited third party.

---

## What this strategy can and cannot claim

The audit measures **what the category says**, not what consumers believe. It is a
supply-side reading of 288 advertisements, and it is strong evidence about competitive
behaviour.

It is **not** consumer research. The barrier diagnosis in Part 1 is therefore an inference
from category behaviour, stated as such. Confirming it needs a claims-attitude study, which
we would recommend as the campaign's first tracking wave rather than a precondition for
starting.

This is flagged here rather than buried, because a strategy that overstates its evidence is
easy to dismantle in the room.

## Part 1 — Communication objective

### The business context

Pakistan's life insurance market has reached **3% of the population**. Penetration is
**0.67% of GDP** against a global average of 6.7%; density is **US$14 per head** against
India's US$82 (Competition Commission of Pakistan, 2025).

**State Life holds 55% of that market** (CCP, 2025).

Those two facts together determine the strategy, and they point somewhere unusual. In a
market this under-penetrated, **the competitor is not another insurer. It is
non-consumption.** And because State Life holds the majority share, **the majority of any
category growth accrues to State Life.** Fighting for share is the wrong war for the
brand that has already won it.

### The communication objective

> **From:** *Life insurance is a promise you cannot check, sold by an industry that may
> not pay.*
>
> **To:** *State Life publishes what it settles — so the promise is checkable before you
> need it.*

### The barrier

Of the five standard barriers — awareness, understanding, belief, relevance, salience —
the evidence points to **belief**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Not awareness** | State Life is the 55% market leader and a household institution. Reach is not the missing input. |
| **Not salience** | Category output is high; brands post several times a week. Presence exists. |
| **Belief** | **233 of 288 advertisements (81%) carry no proof of anything.** Only **14 of 288 (5%)** address the category's core objection. Three advertisements in 288 cite a performance number. |

**A category whose advertising proves nothing has an audience with nothing to believe.**

The strategic consequence is concrete: **more reach will not fix this.** A budget spent on
frequency against an unbelieved promise buys repetition, not conversion. The money belongs
behind proof.

## Part 2 — Target audience

### Primary audience — the considered non-buyer

Adults who **know what life insurance is, could afford a policy, and have not bought one.**
In a market at 3% penetration this is not a niche; it is almost everybody.

They are defined by attitude and behaviour, not by demographics: they have encountered the
category — through a bank, an agent, a relative — and declined it. They are not uninformed.
They are unconvinced.

**Audience insight:** *They do not doubt that they should protect their family. They doubt
that the institution will be there when the family needs it — and nobody has ever shown them
otherwise.*

That is a tension, not a census fact. It is uncomfortable for the category, and it is
precisely what 81% of category advertising declines to address.

### The decision-making unit

In life insurance the buyer, the payer and the beneficiary are usually **three different
people**, and category advertising talks almost exclusively to the first.

| Role | Who | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer / payer** | Usually the earning head of household | A reason to believe the promise holds |
| **Influencer** | Spouse, parent, adult child | Permission — that this is prudence, not pessimism |
| **Beneficiary** | Spouse and children | Nothing at purchase; everything at claim |

**Strategic implication:** the beneficiary is the person the promise is *about* and the
person who eventually tests it. *Policyholder's Voices* — State Life's existing testimonial
series — already speaks in the beneficiary's voice. That asset is pointed at the right
person and is currently running as two posts in twenty-four.

### Two audiences the category has abandoned

Both are evidenced in the audit, and both are uncontested.

**The over-60s.** **Two advertisements in 288 are cast for anyone over 60. Both are State
Life's.** This is the segment with the assets, the dependants, the maturing policies and
the nearest claim — and the segment a rewards app structurally cannot serve, which is why
the three brands converging on apps have vacated it.

**Urdu-first Pakistan.** **204 of 208 captioned advertisements in the category are
Latin-script only; four carry any Urdu.** A category that has reached 3% of the population
is advertising in the language of the minority it has already reached. State Life's own
heritage line — *Aye Khuda Meray Abbu Salamat Rahain* — is Urdu. The asset and the gap
are the same thing.

### Who we are not talking to

**The app-native rewards audience.** Jubilee drives 79% of its output into Jubilee Active;
EFU 50% across three ecosystems; Adamjee 33%. **State Life is at 0% and should stay there.**

That is not a concession, it is the strategy: those brands are building apps because
bancassurance means **the bank owns their customer and rations their access to it** (CCP,
2025). State Life distributes through a tied agency force. **The app solves a problem State
Life does not have**, and competing there would spend a leader's budget on a challenger's
weakness.

## Part 3 — Communication approach

> **Category-building, led by proof.**
>
> Grow the category rather than fight for share — because at 55% State Life captures the
> majority of any growth — and grow it by removing the belief barrier no competitor is
> addressing.

**Why category-building and not recruitment.** A challenger takes share; a leader grows the
pool. With 97% of the population outside the category and State Life holding 55% inside it,
every point of penetration is worth more to State Life than to anyone else. Recruitment
campaigns against EFU or Jubilee would fight over 3% of the country.

**Why proof and not awareness.** Awareness is not the barrier (Part 1). Proof is the input
the category has withheld for so long that supplying it is now differentiating in itself.

**What we are explicitly not doing:**

- Not building a rewards ecosystem
- Not competing on discounts, partner offers or lifestyle content
- Not running heritage as the lead claim — 29 of 288 advertisements already claim national
  trust, and 22 of those sit in the pride register. Heritage is a **support**, not a spine
- Not leading in English

## Part 4 — Messaging direction

### Positioning statement

> For **Pakistanis who believe in protecting their families but not in the institutions that
> promise it**, **State Life** is the **life insurer owned by the state** that **publishes
> what it settles**, because **a public grievance obligation makes its record checkable in a
> way no private competitor's is.**

### Message architecture

**MASTER MESSAGE** — *State Life publishes what it settles.*

| Pillar | The claim | Proof point (fact, not adjective) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. We settle, and we publish it** | Our record is public and repeated | **E-Kachehri, published twice: 745 complaints received / 744 resolved (13 Aug 2026); 730 / 729 (22 Jul 2026)** |
| **2. We are answerable by law** | Our accountability is a duty, not a campaign | E-Kachehri is a **statutory grievance-redress forum**, chaired by the CEO. It happens whether or not marketing wants it |
| **3. We are underwritten by Pakistan** | Our promise has the state behind it | **State-owned. 55% of the life insurance market** (CCP, 2025). Operating since **1972** |

**Why these three and not four.** Each is distinct: pillar 1 is the record, pillar 2 is why
the record exists, pillar 3 is why it can be relied on. None can be merged, and none can be
copied — a private insurer can publish an annual claims total, but it cannot manufacture a
recurring public complaints forum, because it has no obligation that creates one.

### Message by audience — one proposition, different proof

| Audience | Lead pillar | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Considered non-buyer | 1 | The number. Checkable, recent, repeated |
| Influencer (spouse, parent) | 2 | Answerability — this is prudence, not pessimism |
| Over-60s / maturing policyholders | 1 | *Claim what is yours* — the promise, honoured now |
| Existing policyholders | 3 | Reassurance and advocacy |
| Rural / Urdu-first | 3 then 1 | The state's own insurer, in the audience's own language |

**The proposition does not change by audience. Only the proof does.** A strategy with a
different promise per segment is three strategies.

### One caution on evidence

Pillar 1 currently rests on **complaints resolved**, which is what State Life publishes
today. It is a strong number — it measures behaviour when a customer pushes back — but it is
not a **claims-paid** figure.

If State Life can also publish claims paid and settlement turnaround, pillar 1 becomes
materially stronger and directly outranks Jubilee's PKR 54 billion. **That is a data request,
not an assumption**, and it should be raised at briefing rather than written into creative.

## Part 5 — Communication journey

A standard awareness → consideration → conversion funnel assumes awareness is the barrier.
It is not. The journey is therefore **barrier-led**, and each stage clears one obstacle
between hearing the promise and trusting it.

| Stage | Job | Lead message | Proof at this stage | Channel role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hear it** | Register that an insurer is making a checkable claim | Master message | The number, stated plainly | **Broadcast + press** — reach and register at scale |
| **2. Believe it** | Move from claim to conviction | Pillar 1 | *Policyholder's Voices* — named claimants, in their own words | **Long-form video, radio testimonial** |
| **3. Check it** | Let them verify rather than take our word | Pillar 2 | E-Kachehri: a public forum they can attend | **Digital, PR, press** — where a record is read as a record |
| **4. Act on it** | Convert belief into an application | Pillar 3 | State ownership; the tied agency force | **Agency network, call centre, branch** |
| **5. Tell someone** | Make the settled claim visible | Pillar 1 | The next published figure | **Owned channels, earned PR** |

**What moves someone between stages** — the argument, not the arrows:

- **1 → 2** requires a *person*, not a statistic. A number registers; a beneficiary
  convinces. This is why *Policyholder's Voices* is the campaign's second wave, not its
  garnish.
- **2 → 3** requires *falsifiability*. A claim you are invited to check is believed
  differently from one you are asked to accept.
- **3 → 4** requires *availability*. State Life's tied agency force is the conversion asset
  — the thing bancassurance-dependent competitors cannot match.
- **4 → 5** requires the *cadence*. The figure republished is what turns a campaign into a
  standard the category is measured against.

**The mechanic that makes the journey repeat:** the E-Kachehri resolution figure is
published every cycle. Stage 5 becomes the next stage 1. The platform generates work rather
than repeating a launch.

### How we would know it is working

Directional, not a media plan — measurement design belongs with the media submission:

| Stage | Signal |
|---|---|
| Hear it | Prompted awareness of the claim, not just the brand |
| Believe it | Shift on a single tracked statement: *"State Life pays what it promises"* |
| Check it | E-Kachehri attendance and enquiry volume |
| Act on it | Agency lead volume and first-year applications |
| Tell someone | Referral share of new business |

## Assumptions stated

The tender's **Section VII, Schedule of Requirements, is referenced at ITA 6.8 but absent
from the issued document**, and the clarification window has closed. No product line,
audience or objective was specified.

We have therefore written the strategy at **corporate brand level**, with individual life
and retirement as the first product applications — the two lines where the audit shows the
category's attention is weakest. A brand-level strategy can carry any product beneath it; a
product-level one cannot be widened.
