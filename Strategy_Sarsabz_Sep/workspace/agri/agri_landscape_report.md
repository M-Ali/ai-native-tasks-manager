# Pakistan's agricultural landscape: crops, seasons, land and the fertilizer opportunity

A background report for campaign planning. 16 September 2026.

**No company or brand is named in this document.** It describes the market, not any participant in it.

---

## How to read this

Every figure below is either **measured** (published by a statistical agency) or **modelled** (arithmetic done here from
measured inputs, and labelled as such). Nothing is estimated from experience or industry talk.

| Grade | Meaning | Example in this report |
|---|---|---|
| Measured | Taken directly from an official table | Cropped area, nutrient consumption, crop area and production |
| Reconciled | Taken from one official source and checked against another | Province crop areas, checked against the national table |
| Modelled | Arithmetic performed here on measured inputs | Crops per year, nutrient use per hectare, the phosphate gap |
| Not available | Asked for and not found in a reachable source | Sowing and harvest months |

Two cautions that matter more than any single number:

- **Crop-wise fertilizer consumption in the national statistics is an assumption, not a measurement.** The published
  crop-wise table allocates the national total by fixed percentages (wheat 50%, cotton 25%, rice 12%, sugarcane 8%,
  maize 1.5%, others 9.5%) — the same percentages every year. It cannot be used to prove what any crop actually consumes.
- **The published "crop calendars" are administrative, not agronomic.** They are the dates on which crop estimates are
  released, not sowing or harvest windows.

## 1. The land

Measured, 2024-25 unless stated.

| | Pakistan | Punjab | Sindh | Khyber Pakhtunkhwa | Balochistan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total cropped area (m ha) | 24.60 | 17.52 | 3.90 | 1.73 | 1.45 |
| Share of national cropped area | 100% | 71.2% | 15.9% | 7.0% | 5.9% |
| Net area sown (m ha) | 16.20 | 10.79 | 2.83 | 1.15 | 1.43 |
| Area sown more than once (m ha) | 8.40 | 6.73 | 1.07 | 0.58 | 0.02 |

**Two provinces are the agricultural economy.** Punjab and Sindh together are 87% of the cropped area. Everything that
follows concentrates on them.

**Irrigation differs fundamentally between them** (measured, 2024-25, million hectares irrigated):

| | Total irrigated | Government canals | Tubewells | Canal + tubewell together |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Punjab | 15.47 | 3.25 | 3.09 | 8.71 |
| Sindh | 1.52 | 1.16 | 0.36 | — |

Punjab's farmer mostly controls his own water: over half his irrigated area is served by canal *and* tubewell, so he can
irrigate when the crop needs it. Sindh's farmer is almost entirely canal-fed, so his timing is set by the canal rotation,
not by his own decision. **This single difference shapes when fertilizer can be applied, and therefore when it is bought.**

## 2. How many crops per year, and where

Modelled: total cropped area ÷ net area sown. A figure of 1.00 means one crop a year on the land that is sown; 2.00 means
the same land carries two crops a year.

| | Crops per year, 2024-25 |
|---|---|
| Pakistan | 1.52 |
| **Punjab** | **1.62** |
| **Sindh** | **1.38** |
| Khyber Pakhtunkhwa | 1.50 |
| Balochistan | 1.01 |

**Punjab turns its land over 17% more often than Sindh.** That is the difference between a farmer who buys fertilizer for
roughly three crops every two years and one who buys for about four crops every three years.

### Within Punjab, the range is far wider than the provincial average

District-level land-use data exists for Punjab (2018-19, the latest district block published):

| Intensity | Districts |
|---|---|
| **Highest** | Kasur 3.84, Hafizabad 2.34, Muzaffargarh 2.05, Chiniot 2.04, Gujranwala 2.03, Nankana Sahib 2.02, Multan 1.99 |
| **Around the provincial average** | Bahawalpur 1.82, Okara 1.80, Khanewal 1.78, Gujrat 1.77, Sialkot 1.76 |
| **Lowest** | Islamabad 1.00, Khushab 1.05, Chakwal 1.07, Attock 1.10, Layyah 1.29 |

By division: Gujranwala 1.85, Lahore 1.84, Multan 1.83, Sahiwal 1.72, Bahawalpur 1.59, Dera Ghazi Khan 1.50,
Faisalabad 1.48, Rawalpindi 1.22.

**A district sown nearly four times a year is a different customer from one sown once.** Kasur buys roughly four times as
often per hectare as Chakwal. Equivalent district data for Sindh is not published in the same series.

## 4. Cropping systems and the season

Pakistan runs **two seasons**: **Rabi from October to March** (wheat is the dominant crop) and **Kharif from April to
September** (rice, cotton, sugarcane and maize). Sugarcane occupies the ground for 12-18 months and therefore sits across
both.

The land is organised into crop production regions with settled rotations (measured, FAO 2004):

| Region | Area | System | Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| Punjab I | 5.5m ha | Cotton-wheat | Canal, tubewell |
| Punjab II | 2.8m ha | Rice-wheat | Canal, tubewell |
| Punjab III | 4.1m ha | Mixed crops | Canal, tubewell |
| Punjab IV | 1.9m ha | Pulses-wheat | Canal, rainfed |
| Punjab V | 1.2m ha | Maize/wheat-oilseeds | Rainfed |
| Sindh I | 1.6m ha | Cotton-wheat | Canal |
| Sindh II | 1.1m ha | Rice-wheat | Canal |
| Sindh III | 1.3m ha | Mixed crops | Canal, dry |

**The practical calendar is the rotation, not the crop.** A cotton-wheat farmer makes two fertilizer decisions a year,
roughly six months apart; a rice-wheat farmer makes two on a different clock; a rainfed pulses-wheat farmer makes one and
may skip it.

### What is not available

**Sowing and harvest months are not in any source reachable for this report.** The published crop calendars are
estimate-release dates; the national agricultural statistics publication carries no sowing windows; the provincial crop
reporting service's crop life calendar could not be retrieved (its host did not resolve); and the agricultural survey
chapter discusses sowing *decisions* without giving dates. Month-level windows circulate on commercial websites, but
nothing there is traceable to an official source, so they are deliberately omitted.

**To close this gap:** the provincial crop reporting services publish a crop life calendar by crop and district. That
single document would convert the season-level calendar above into a month-level one.

## 5. Water is the calendar that can be evidenced

Measured.

| | Kharif (Apr-Sep) | Rabi (Oct-Mar) |
|---|---|---|
| National water availability | 60.6 MAF (2025) | 31.4 MAF (2025-26) |
| Punjab canal withdrawals | 31.88 MAF | 15.53 MAF |
| Sindh canal withdrawals | 26.16 MAF | 12.14 MAF |

Average system usage is 103.5 MAF a year, against 92.0 MAF in the latest two seasons combined.

**Kharif carries roughly twice the water of Rabi — but Rabi grows wheat**, which is the largest single crop and,
by the national allocation assumption, the largest single consumer of fertilizer. The season with less water carries the
crop with more demand. Sindh's rabi share is particularly tight: 12.14 MAF for 2.83m hectares of net sown land.

Groundwater is the relief valve in Punjab and not in Sindh: the agricultural survey records that in Sindh "aquifers are
largely brackish, limiting usable supplies", and that Indus Basin extraction increasingly exceeds recharge.

## 6. The fertilizer market as it is today

Measured, 2024-25, in thousand nutrient tonnes.

| | Nitrogen | Phosphate | Potash | Total | Share of national |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pakistan** | 3,362.5 | 917.1 | 44.6 | **4,324.2** | 100% |
| **Punjab** | 2,299.7 | 653.1 | 34.1 | **2,986.9** | 69.1% |
| **Sindh** | 789.9 | 193.0 | 8.1 | **991.0** | 22.9% |
| Khyber Pakhtunkhwa | 148.4 | 33.4 | 1.5 | 183.3 | 4.2% |
| Balochistan | 124.5 | 37.6 | 0.9 | 163.0 | 3.8% |

**Two provinces are 92% of the market.**

Modelled — nutrient use per cropped hectare, and the nutrient ratio:

| | kg per cropped hectare | N : P₂O₅ : K₂O |
|---|---|---|
| Pakistan | 175.8 | 1 : 0.27 : 0.013 |
| Punjab | 170.5 | 1 : 0.28 : 0.015 |
| Sindh | **254.2** | 1 : 0.24 : 0.010 |
| Khyber Pakhtunkhwa | 106.2 | 1 : 0.23 : 0.010 |
| Balochistan | 112.0 | 1 : 0.30 : 0.007 |

**Sindh applies 49% more per hectare than Punjab and is the least balanced of the two.** Sindh's problem is not how much
it applies; it is what it applies.

### What is moving right now

Measured, July-March FY2026: total nutrient offtake 3,795 thousand tonnes, **up 11.4%** year on year — but the movement is
one-sided:

- **Nitrogen +14.8%**
- **Phosphate −1.9%**, which the agricultural survey attributes directly to high prices
- Potash +26.2%, from a base so small it changes nothing

The same source records the farmer's arithmetic plainly: **every additional Rs 100 per 50 kg bag puts about Rs 20 billion
more on farmers.** Phosphate is the nutrient that gets dropped when money is tight, and it is being dropped now.

## 7. The size of the opportunity

Three separate opportunities, sized by different methods. Each is directional: none is a forecast, and each states its
own basis.

### a. The phosphate gap against official recommendations — the largest single opportunity

Provincial research institutes issue fertilizer recommendations by crop and zone. Comparing those recommendations with
recorded application rates, across the crop-province pairs where both are documented, and multiplying by the areas in
section 3:

| Crop and province | Area ('000 ha) | Recommended P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | Applied P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | Gap (kg/ha) | Gap ('000 nutrient t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wheat, Punjab | 6,480.5 | 85 | 30 | 55 | 356 |
| Rice, Punjab | 2,188.6 | 67 | 20 | 47 | 103 |
| Cotton, Punjab | 1,485.2 | 60 | 6.5 | 53.5 | 80 |
| Rice, Sindh | 603.3 | 83.5 | 20 | 63.5 | 38 |
| Sugarcane, Sindh | 289.4 | 112.5 | 27.5 | 85 | 25 |
| **Total** | | | | | **602** |

**On five crop-province pairs alone, the shortfall against official recommendations is about 602 thousand nutrient
tonnes of phosphate — two-thirds the size of the entire phosphate market as it stands (917 thousand tonnes).**

Basis and caution: recommendation and application rates come from a 2004 national study, the most recent source found
with both figures side by side; crop areas are 2022-23. The gap is therefore indicative of scale and direction, not a
current-year quantity. It is corroborated by the fact that the national nutrient structure has barely moved in twenty
years: that study recorded a ratio of 1 : 0.28 : 0.01 and nitrogen at 78% of nutrients; the 2024-25 figures are
1 : 0.27 : 0.013 and 77.8%.

### b. Balanced nutrition — the ratio nobody is selling

The generally accepted balanced ratio between N, P₂O₅ and K₂O is **1 : 0.5 : 0.5**. Pakistan applies 1 : 0.27 : 0.013.

- Phosphate runs at roughly **half** the balanced level.
- Potash is **1.8 kg per cropped hectare** nationally — effectively absent.
- Fewer than **2% of farmers** apply potash at all; about **5%** apply micronutrients.

The documented consequences of sustained imbalance are lower fertilizer use efficiency, yield reduction, lower farmer
income, soil mining of phosphate and potash, falling response ratios, and higher nitrogen losses to the environment.
**Every one of those is visible to the farmer as a worse crop, and none of it is currently being explained to him by
anyone selling him a bag.**

### c. The unfertilized and under-fertilized farmer

- **92% of farmers apply nitrogen, 83% apply phosphate** — but in rainfed areas, barely **50%** of farmers use fertilizer
  at all.
- Rainfed systems are a defined part of the map: Punjab IV (1.9m ha, canal and rainfed) and Punjab V (1.2m ha, rainfed).
- Per hectare, Pakistan applies **160.3 kg** against India's 199.1, Bangladesh's 391.9 and China's 394.0.

**Closing only the gap to India would require roughly a quarter more nutrient per hectare than Pakistan uses today.**

### Where each product type has a claim

| Product type | Where the opening is | The argument it can make |
|---|---|---|
| Phosphatic compounds (NP and similar) | Phosphate offtake is falling on price while nitrogen rises | A cheaper route to phosphate per acre than the standard high-analysis product |
| Nitrate-based nitrogen (CAN and similar) | Sindh's mix is the most nitrogen-skewed, on canal-timed irrigation where split applications matter | The form Europe defaults to, suited to timed splits on irrigated land |
| Urea and DAP | The volume market, decided by price and availability | Availability and authenticity rather than persuasion |
| Potash and micronutrients | Adoption below 2%, use at 1.8 kg per hectare | Balanced nutrition — an argument no one in the category is making |

## 8. What is practised internationally, and what Pakistan does differently

| Practice | The international position | Pakistan's position |
|---|---|---|
| **Application rate** | Bangladesh 391.9 and China 394.0 kg per hectare of arable land; India 199.1 (2023) | 160.3 kg — below the global average of about 154 only in the sense of being near it, and far below its neighbours |
| **Balance** | Generally accepted ratio 1 : 0.5 : 0.5 | 1 : 0.27 : 0.013, and structurally unchanged since the late 1990s |
| **Nitrogen form** | Ammonium nitrate and calcium ammonium nitrate are the European norm, "well suited to most European soils and climatic conditions"; urea's market share is "high in Asia while it is lower in the EU and in North America" | Urea dominates; nitrate-based nitrogen is a minority form |
| **Practice framework** | The 4R framework since 2009: "the right source (or product) at the right rate, right time and right place" | No equivalent framework is in general extension use; farmers seek this advice informally |

Globally, nitrogen is 108 million tonnes of nutrient — 60% of all fertilizer — of which urea alone is 60 million tonnes.
**Urea's dominance is global; its dominance of Pakistan's mix is what makes the ratio unbalanced.**

## 9. Data limits, stated plainly

1. **Sowing and harvest months are absent.** Season and rotation are evidenced; month-level windows are not.
2. **Crop-wise fertilizer consumption is an allocation rule**, not a measurement.
3. **Application rates by crop are twenty years old.** The structure is corroborated by current ratios, but a current
   crop-level survey does not appear to be public.
4. **District land-use data exists for Punjab only**, and the latest published block is 2018-19.
5. **Minor crops are excluded** because province attribution could not be validated.
6. **Provincial nutrient figures are consumption, not sales by channel.** They cannot be read as market share.

### What to obtain next

- The provincial crop life calendar, by crop and district, for month-level timing.
- A current crop-level fertilizer use survey, to replace the 2004 rates.
- District land-use data for Sindh, to match the Punjab intensity map.
- Soil test data by district, which would convert the balanced-ratio argument from national to field level.
