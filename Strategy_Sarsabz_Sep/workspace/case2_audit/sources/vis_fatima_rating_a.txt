RATING RATIONALE 
Assigned ratings reflect the Company's strong position in Pakistan's fertilizer sector, 
supported by significant operating capacity and a nationwide distribution network. 
Despite decline in overall sales volume, t opline increased in CY24 on the back of 
higher product prices . However, net sales decreased in 1QCY25 due to a delayed 
Kharif season. Profit margins improved significantly during the period under review, 
on account of higher prices of fertilizer products. Debt servicing coverage ratios 
declined in the period under review  amid increased taxation and  higher debt 
repayments. Borrowings increased, mainly to finance increased working capital and 
funding long-term investments, slightly elevating capitalization ratios, though 
maintaining a robust portfolio of liquid assets provided significant cover against 
these borrowings, largely mitigating financial risk. 
 
Looking ahead, assigned ratings will hinge on maintaining operational efficiencies, 
improving market presence, recovery in sales volumes, while maintaining sound  
coverages and a conservative balance sheet.

FINANCIAL RISK 
Capital Structure 
The Company's equity base expanded to Rs. 14 4b (end -CY23 Rs. 11 9b) by end-
CY24 primarily driven by profit retention despite distribution of Rs. 11.5b in total 
dividends in CY24 (CY23: Rs. 11.0b). Equity increased to Rs. 149b by end-1QCY25 
due to sustained profit retention. 
 
FATIMA availed an arbitrage opportunity due to which borrowings increased at 
end-CY24. Long -term borrowings increased notably to Rs. 4 4.3b (end -CY23: Rs. 
8.4b) by end-CY24 and short-term borrowings increased Rs. 19.5b (end -CY23: Rs. 
937.8m). Consequently, capitalization ratios were slightly elevated as gearing 
reached 0.45x (end -CY23: 0.08x) and leverage increased to 1.20x (end -CY23: 
0.96x) by end-CY24. Gearing and leverage declined at end -1QCY25 on account of 
both higher  equity base and slightly lower borrowings. Financial risk is alleviated to 
a larger extent as FATIMA maintained substantial liquid assets amounting Rs. 70.6b 
(end-CY24: Rs. 40.1b, end -CY23: Rs. 26.3b) by end -1QCY25, providing cover 
against borrowings with virtually nil gearing based in net debt. 
 
While no major capex is expected in the outgoing year, the Company plans to 
diversify into different ventures  and may inject funds into subsidiaries during the 
rating horizon. Overall, the  management expects borrowings to decrease going 
forward. 
 
Profitability 
Despite lower volumes, net sales increased by 9.1%  (CY24: Rs. 256.9b, CY23: Rs. 
235.4b) due to higher prices.  While volumetric sales of Urea stabilized in CY24 
compared CY23, offtake of DAP, CAN and NP declined by 48%, 15% and 19% 
respectively. Overall, sales volumes reduced by 12%  in CY24 compared to CY23  
amid slowdown in market demand.  
 
Urea, NP and CAN formed majority of Company’s sales, in line with historical levels, 
though management’s focus on NP is evident as it comprises average 48.8% of sales 
in terms of revenue over the past three years.

In CY24, gross margin improved to 35.7% (CY23: 31.5%) due to higher selling 
prices. Other income increased to Rs. 12.5b (CY23: Rs. 7.3b) in CY24 due to higher 
profits on investments and savings account and gains on short -term investments. 
Financial charges were largely unchanged during CY24 compared to CY23. As a 
result of higher gross margin and increased other income, net profit increased 
notably to Rs. 36.4b (CY23: Rs. 23.0b) leading to an improved net margin of 14.2% 
(CY23: 9.8%) in CY24.  
 
In 1QCY25, demand was hampered by delayed onset of Kharif season. 
Subsequently, fertilizer offtake reduced industrywide. FATIMA’s topline declined 
by 34.2% to Rs. 43.0b in 1QCY25 compared to SPLY. Margins continued to improve 
on account of favorable pricing factor, lower operating costs and healthy earnings 
from other income. Gross margin clocked in at 43.8% (1QCY24: 41.3%) and net 
margin improved to 17.5% (1QCY24: 12.5%) in 1QCY25.  Management projects a 
topline contraction in CY25, owing to  prevailing market unpredictability and 
challenging farmer economics. Neve rtheless, margins are forecasted to remain 
intact.

increased to 1.61x (end-CY23: 1.42x) at end-CY24, mainly reflecting the impact of
