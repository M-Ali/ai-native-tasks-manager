# Sarsabz — brand audit (Case 2), working draft

Compiled 15 September 2026. **Status: complete for the pitch draft.** Evidence streams: owned YouTube (24 months, coded),
farmer conversation (1,433 comments, `comments/analysis.md`), the competitor scan (Engro coded on the same schema, presence swept
for FFC, Bubber Sher and Agritech, §3.8), and category structure plus Sarsabz Ki Jeet verified from primary and independent sources.
What follows from it is in `big_idea.md` (the Big Idea) and `concept/the_concept.md` (the campaign). All of it is in
`workspace/out/Sarsabz_case2_deck-v4.pptx`. Findings that need Facebook or TikTok content (not yet coded) are marked *provisional*.
Every figure carries its source; register at the end.

## How to read this

- **Retrieved** = read by us from the document itself. **Reported** = from a secondary summary. Marked where it matters.
- YouTube views on a brand channel are mostly **bought**. They are used here as evidence of *where Sarsabz puts
  media weight*, never as organic interest and never as share of voice.
- Owned content was coded from thumbnail, title and description on the `category-creative-scan` schema
  (`owned/codes_window.csv`). Videos were not watched end to end.
- The owned read is supply side (what the brand says). The comment corpus is demand side (what farmers ask), but it's
  self-selected viewers of agronomy channels and can't support shares or "most farmers" claims.

## 1. The finding in one sentence

**Sarsabz spends most heavily on its yield promise, but proves it almost nowhere, and it's answering a question farmers aren't
asking: the 10% sells NP + CAN, categories Fatima already owns, so the real rival is the DAP + urea habit, and what farmers
actually ask is how to switch without wasting what they buy.**

## 2. The mechanism: the promise sits on products Fatima owns

| | Fatima Group share | Substitutes it competes with | Price gap | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NP** (Nitrophos) | 93% (Fatima Fertilizer 51% + Pak Arab 42%), 2022-23; ">90%" 2025 | DAP (FFC 61%, Engro 21%) | NP ~PKR 4,000/bag below DAP (3-yr, widened from ~2,200) | CCP Table 11 (retrieved); BMA Jan 2026 (reported, broker) |
| **CAN** | Pak Arab 38% + Fatima Fertilizer (cell blank in source; implied 62%) = sole producer | Urea (FFC 44%, Engro 29%, Fatima 13%) | CAN PKR 250-300/bag below urea (10-yr) | CCP Table 11 (retrieved); BMA (reported) |
| **Urea** | 13% (2022-23) → ~17% (CY25) | — | — | CCP; BMA (reported) |

Three consequences for the audit:

1. **The tactical campaign is not a brand-share fight. It is a product-system switch.** A farmer persuaded by "10% se bhi
   ziyada" has no other NP or CAN brand to buy. Whatever Sona or Heera advertise about urea and DAP, the argument Sarsabz has to
   win is *"NP + CAN instead of DAP + urea."* The brand guidelines' competitive frame (Sona = reliability, Heera =
   transformation, p.23 of the brief) is the right frame for Sarsabz Urea and DAP and for Bubber Sher. It is the wrong frame for this campaign.
2. **Price and yield point the same way, and the communication uses only one.** NP is materially cheaper than DAP and CAN
   cheaper than urea. None of the 16 functional uploads in 24 months mentions price, cost or saving in its title or description
   (text search; voiceovers not checked). "More yield *and* a cheaper bag"
   is the complete switching argument; the brand is making half of it.
3. **The business challenge is volume stability, not share.** NP offtake fell from 902k to 792k tonnes (CY22→CY23) before recovering
   to 931k in CY24; CAN 826k → 820k → 888k (NFDC via VIS, retrieved). Specialty volumes swing with the farmer's cash position and
   the DAP price. A switching argument that holds when DAP is cheap is the job.

*Caveat on the CCP study:* it is watermarked **DRAFT** (July 2025) and uses NFDC 2022-23 data. NFDC's own 2023-24 review
could not be downloaded (link returns HTML). Ask the client for current product-level share.

## 3. What Sarsabz is actually saying: 24 months of owned YouTube

90 uploads, 14 Oct 2024 – 14 Sep 2026, 164.1M views. Full tables: `owned/owned_findings.md`.

### 3.1 The yield promise gets the money

| | uploads | views | share of views |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Yield / product-quality claim | 16 (18%) | 71.6M | **44%** |
| Brand goodwill (Ramzan, UNDP, Sultans) | 25 (28%) | 47.9M | 29% |
| Farmer recognition (Salam Kissan) | 12 (13%) | 15.7M | 10% |
| Prizes / lucky draw | 4 | 12.4M | 8% |
| Women farmers (Tabeer, Women's Day) | 8 | 9.4M | 6% |
| National identity (Dil Se Dekho / Dil Se Sarsabz) | 22 (24%) | 7.1M | 4% |

The brief asks for "the ability to balance emotional storytelling with product superiority" (p.14). By media weight the
balance is already there. **What's missing is proof, not emphasis.**

### 3.2 Almost none of that weight carries proof

- **10 of the 11 "10% se bhi ziyada" films offer nothing but the badge.** The same execution (a young farmer walking a field, a
  "10 feesad" roundel, three bags) is re-cut for wheat, maize, sugarcane, cotton and rice. The claim is asserted for five crops;
  the brief says it rests on 500+ demo plots (p.5), and no film shows one.
- **Only 5 of 90 uploads answer the switching objection with a real farmer's result.** Four are Sarsabz Ki Jeet (wheat), one is
  Rameez Akbar (rice, 63 maunds/acre, Shikarpur). The rice documentary behind that teaser exists; its teaser drew 1.65M views.
- **Proof devices across all 90:** none 60%, celebrity 27%, awards/competition 6%, expert/partner 3%, farmer testimonial **3%**.

### 3.3 The long cut, where proof would fit, is not being funded

| crop | 15-second cut | 56-60-second cut, same message | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| sugarcane | 5,094,386 | 1,829 | 2,785× |
| maize | 4,211,198 | 2,680 | 1,571× |
| cotton | 8,856,043 | 9,851 | 899× |
| rice | 10,186,761 | 15,966 | 638× |

The 15-second cut is bought; the long cut is left to find its own audience and doesn't. That's a rational media choice for
reminder advertising. It also means **the only format with room for a field comparison, a farmer and a number has
effectively no audience.** The tactical campaign has to make proof fit short formats, or buy the long ones.

### 3.4 "Jeet" means three different things

The brand's most repeated word is split across three unrelated ideas in the same 24 months:

- **"Sarsabz Ki Jeet"**: a farmer's yield win in a government competition (26.1M views)
- **"Nitrophos ko laga aur jeeta ja"**: a scratch-code lucky draw for an Umrah ticket, motorcycle and phone (11.4M)
- **"Jeet ka naam, Sarsabz Ke Sultan"**: a cricket team (22 uploads, 10.1M)

A farmer watching all three learns that with Sarsabz you win a competition, a raffle or a cricket match. Only the first is
evidence for the product. **This is the clearest brand-consistency finding in the audit** and a direct answer to the brief's
consistency question (p.13).

### 3.5 Who the brand talks to, by volume and by weight

- **46% of uploads are cast young (Gen Z, inferred from casting): Multan Sultans and the Dil Se Dekho UGC contest. They
  carry 10% of views.** Most of the channel's output by count is low-cost, youth-facing template and contest content.
- The weight goes to farmer-aged casting: the yield films and Ki Jeet. The core target in the guidelines is male farmers
  25-55 with 12-150+ acres (p.23).
- **Salam Kissan 2025 has already turned urban:** a food blogger (Ali Rehman, 4.0M) and an urban woman at a dining table
  ("behind every meal", 5.4M). That's relevant to Case 1's "rural and urban" ask and worth crediting.
- **The engagement assets are invisible.** The Sarsabz Pakistan App (800K+ downloads, p.7) and Sarsabz AgriMart appear in 3
  uploads with under 2,000 views combined.

### 3.6 Identity drift across signatures *(provisional: needs the Facebook/TikTok read)*

On thumbnails alone the brand signs as **Fatima**, **Fatima Fertilizer**, **Sarsabz**, **Sarsabz Pakistan** and
**Sarsabz Fertilizers**. It also carries separate marks for Salam Kissan, Sarsabz Tabeer and Multan Sultans. Women's Day 2025
is signed Fatima Fertilizer; Women's Day 2026 is signed Sarsabz Tabeer. Two lines are in use: "Zameen ki Jaan, Fasal ki
Shaan" (11 of 90 descriptions, mostly the product films) and the guideline essence "Feeding the Soil, Fueling the Future"
(in no title or description across all 401 uploads).

### 3.7 What farmers ask when the brand isn't in the room

1,433 comments on independent agronomy videos (full analysis: `comments/analysis.md`).

- **Nobody questions whether NP + CAN work. They ask how to use them without wasting them.** Mixing and compatibility is the
  largest subject (220 comments; 83 about NP or CAN; 33 put Nitrophos beside Engro Zabardast urea). *"Zabrdast urea ma zink ha
  aur np ma phosphorus ha to akthy dalen gy to zink phosphorus to fix ho jay ge"* (Zabardast has zinc, NP has phosphorus;
  together they lock up).
- **The switching question is asked in farmers' own words.** (Translated) "How many bags of Sarsabz Nitrophos give the same
  result as one DAP, or can it not replace DAP?" Third parties answer it, inconsistently.
- **The products are discussed; the brand isn't.** CAN 193 comments, NP 147, **Sarsabz 15**, Fatima 4. **Engro Zabardast (89)
  is the only fertilizer brand farmers name routinely.** One farmer asks whether the better product is *"SAR sbz ya Fatima wali"*
  (Sarsabz or the Fatima one).
- **Fatima's own field practice contradicts the creators, and farmers notice.** *"Fatima fertilizer walon ne NP+CAN+ZINC SULFATE
  mila ke chatta kiya hai result bhe behar hai aor aap bol rahai in ko aapus main na milaain"* (Fatima's people mixed NP + CAN +
  zinc sulphate and the result was better, and you say don't mix them).
- **Farmers suspect paid advice** (*"Medicine mafia se paise lekar video banner rehta"*: takes money from the pesticide mafia to make videos). Sponsored creator content
  starts with a credibility deficit.

**What this changes:** the tactical campaign's job is less "believe the 10%" than **"switch safely": what replaces DAP, in what
quantity, mixed with what, on which irrigation, on your soil.** The communication has none of this; the farmer is asking for all of it.

### 3.8 The competition, on the same schema

**Where each brand's audience is** (presence sweep, 15 Sep 2026): Sarsabz is the largest owned audience in the category, with
1.2M on TikTok, 396K on YouTube and 274K on Facebook. Engro has 157K, 81K and 152K. **FFC Sona, the urea leader, has 71K on Facebook
and no official YouTube channel**; its ads appear only on third-party channels. **Bubber Sher and Agritech Tara have no official
channel at all.** The "Bubber Sher" Facebook page is a rock band. **Reach is not Sarsabz's problem.**

**Engro, 110 uploads in the same 24 months, coded identically** (`scan/scan_findings.md`):

| | Sarsabz | Engro |
|---|---:|---:|
| Uploads answering a farmer's objection | **6%** | **58%** |
| Named expert on screen | 0 | 31 |
| Primary claim: advisory / crop advice | 3% | 40% |
| Proof device: none | 60% | 33% |
| Drives to owned app, centre or service | 5 | 28 |
| Long-form (5 min+) | 4 | 11 |
| Cost-saving claim ("Kam Kharch, Zyada Paidawar") | 0 | 6 |
| Authenticity claim (DAP Bharosa Seal, SMS check) | 0 | 3 |
| Yield claim | 15 | 0 |

- **Engro owns advice.** Expert crop tips by named agronomists (Engro Kahanian), soil testing (Mitti Mahir), a podcast on cost per acre
  (GupShup), retail centres (Markaz) and an app. It's almost exactly the demand the farmer comments show.
- **Engro is inside Fatima's categories.** It sells **Engro NP+** and an imported **TSP** as a phosphate alternative to DAP.
- **Engro has taken two territories Sarsabz abandoned.** Sarsabz ran "Sarsabz Ki Asli Nitrophos" (authenticity, 2021-22) and
  "Khaad Muft Hi Samjho" (value, 2017). Neither appears in its last 24 months; Engro now runs both.
- **Nobody claims yield proof on a farmer's field.** Engro makes no yield claim at all. Sarsabz makes it 15 times, and proves it
  in 5 (Ki Jeet and one rice film). **That's the open territory.**

**Ladder placement** (`brand-laddering` skill, computed): Engro sits on Level 3, benefits (mode 3, mean 2.4). Sarsabz is split:
modal Level 5, values, but mean 3.2. **Sarsabz talks at values while Engro occupies benefits, and farmers judge at benefits and below.**

*Limits:* owned YouTube only, one direct competitor with enough owned output to code. FFC's paid TV and Facebook ads aren't
captured; the Meta Ad Library is the next source for them.

## 4. Sarsabz Ki Jeet: can the wins be authentic proof for the yield promise?

**Yes, but only if the claim is cut back to what the competition actually shows. Right now it overclaims in a way a rival or
regulator could challenge.**

What the competition shows, and it's strong:

- **Yields far above normal.** The provincial winners in the films report 69 maunds 38 kg, 69 maunds 35 kg and 69 maunds 29 kg
  per acre (Jhang, Kasur, DG Khan; video descriptions). Pakistan's national wheat yield in 2023-24 was 3,264 kg/ha, about
  **33 maunds/acre** (Economic Survey 2023-24, Table 2.5, provisional). **The winners grew roughly 2.1× the national average.**
- **Breadth.** "40 mein se 34 azlaa mein hui Sarsabz kissanon ki jeet": wins in 34 of 40 districts (video descriptions).
- **Third-party adjudication.** It's a Punjab Agriculture Department competition, and the Department's Secretary co-presented
  the prizes (Dawn advertorial, 11 Nov 2025).

Where it overreaches:

1. **"Sabit kar diya ke... 10% se bhi ziyada paidawaar"** (#41, 7.5M views). A competition win shows a very high yield,
   not a 10% difference against a comparison. The 10% comes from paired demo plots. The film joins two different pieces of evidence and
   presents one as proof of the other. **Oddly, it undersells:** "double the national average" is a bigger and
   more defensible statement than "10%".
2. **"All achieved through the use of Sarsabz Nitrophos and Sarsabz CAN"** (Dawn *advertorial*, paid, 11 Nov 2025: provincial
   top three plus "97 top three district-level positions"). Dawn's **independent** report of 3 Aug 2025 quotes the Chakwal
   district first-place winner describing his inputs as **urea and DAP**. That doesn't contradict "34 of 40 districts". It
   does mean any version saying *all* winners, or naming a district without checking, is exposed.
3. **Inconsistent numbers across channels:** "97 district positions" (press) vs "34 of 40 districts" (films). Pick one, keep
   the source list, and use it everywhere.
4. **It proves wheat in Punjab only.** The same 10% is asserted for rice, cotton, maize and sugarcane with no equivalent proof,
   and Sindh (half the core target, p.23) has no Ki Jeet equivalent.
5. **Verification.** The official results page (agripunjab.gov.pk/wheat-yield-competition-result) couldn't be reached from
   here. The client should hold the input records for every winner cited.

6. **The audience is less impressed than the national average suggests.** In the comment corpus, two farmers report 55-70 and
   63-68 maunds of wheat on DAP + urea, with their inputs. That's thin evidence, but it's a warning: to a progressive farmer, 69.9 maunds
   is a good season, not a miracle.

**Recommendation for the tactical campaign:** use Ki Jeet as *aspiration proof* (named farmers, their district, their maunds per acre).
Carry the 10% on *comparison proof*: a field beside a field, same soil, DAP + urea against NP + CAN. That's the demo-plot evidence
Sarsabz already holds, and it's what farmers ask for. Never let one stand in for the other.

## 5. Answers to the nine things the brief asks for (p.13)

| Brief asks | Position now | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunities to strengthen positioning | Make "Experts" a demonstrated behaviour: be the authority on *how* to use NP + CAN, which farmers ask and creators answer badly | medium-high (coded + comments) |
| Areas of improvement in communication | Proof is missing from 10 of 11 yield films; long cuts unfunded; price argument absent; no usage or mixing guidance | **high** (coded + comments) |
| New creative territories | Proof on a real field, beside the old way: vacant in 200 coded uploads (Engro owns advice, Sarsabz asserts). Taken forward as the Big Idea and the campaign "Dus Feesad Aur. Khet Gawah Hai." | **high** (coded + comments) |
| Emotional + functional connection | Ki Jeet already fuses them (pride + result); extend beyond wheat and Punjab | medium |
| Brand consistency across touchpoints | Reclaim "Jeet" for yield only; one signature; one line; put Sarsabz on the products farmers discuss generically (CAN 193, NP 147, Sarsabz 15) | **high** (coded + comments) |
| Can Ki Jeet be authentic proof | Yes, as aspiration proof with the claim corrected (§4) | **high** (sourced) |
| An identified brand or business challenge | Specialty volumes swing with DAP price; plus TOM 22% vs Competitor 1 at 62% (p.29) | medium |
| Strengthen leadership | Lead the *category* (balanced nutrition, NP + CAN) since Fatima owns it; don't fight on urea terms | medium |
| Engagement with farmers and stakeholders | Farmers are asking creators for WhatsApp numbers and replies; the App (800K) and AgriMart could answer them and collect farmer-logged yields as proof | medium, *needs client data* |

## 7. Questions for the client

- Competitor 1–4 in the brand-health study: who they are, sample, method, fieldwork dates. Is the "6% ahead" TOM claim against Competitor 2 only?
- The demo-plot dataset behind 10%: crops, seasons, provinces, the spread, and whether it has been cleared for advertising substantiation.
- The Ki Jeet winners list with input records; permission terms for using the Punjab Agriculture Department name.
- Product-level share and volume for NP and CAN, 2024-26.
- App data: active users, whether farmers log inputs or yields.
- Fatima's official position on mixing NP with zinc-coated urea, sulphur and potash, and on CAN timing.
- Whether the Nitrophos anti-counterfeit sticker is the same code used for the lucky draw.
- Why Sarsabz CAN and Pak Arab CAN both go to market, and how a farmer should choose.
- Which videos the supplied comments came from, and whether any were Fatima-sponsored.
- Pitch date and budget (neither is in the brief).
