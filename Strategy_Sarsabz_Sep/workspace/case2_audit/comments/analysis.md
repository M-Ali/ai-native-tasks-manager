# Sarsabz — Farmer Conversation Analysis

Analysis base: **1,433 comments analysed** (from 1,531 rows; 499 are replies). Source: `data/COMMENTS.xlsx`, YouTube
comments supplied 15 September 2026. Video titles, channels and dates were not included in the file. From how commenters
address the hosts ("Abid bhai", "Baba ji", "Dr sahib", "@CropReformer"), the videos are **independent agronomy channels**
advising on fertilizer plans, not Sarsabz's own uploads (*inference*). Script mix: 1,025 Roman Urdu/English, 366 Urdu script, 32 mixed.

Quotes are verbatim, spelling as typed; English in brackets. Urdu-script comments are given in translation and marked.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Farmers aren't asking whether NP and CAN work. They're asking whether they can combine them with what they already use.**
  Mixing and compatibility is the largest subject in the file (220 comments). 83 of those are about NP or CAN, and 33 put
  Nitrophos next to **Engro Zabardast urea**. The fear is specific: *"Zabrdast urea ma zink ha aur np ma phosphorus ha to akthy
  dalen gy to zink phosphorus to fix ho jay ge"* (Zabardast has zinc, NP has phosphorus; put them together and they lock up).
  In 1,433 comments nobody questions the yield of NP + CAN. Many are afraid of wasting it.
- **The switching question exists, in farmers' words, and nobody from the brand is answering it.** (Translated from Urdu)
  "How many bags of Sarsabz Nitrophos give the same result as one DAP, or can it not replace DAP?" A reply says three
  bags of Sarsabz Nitrophos beat one and a half of DAP. A rumour goes unchallenged (translated): "people say Nitrophos is
  no use in sandy, soft soil. Is it true?"
- **The brand is nearly invisible; its products are not.** CAN is mentioned in 193 comments and NP in 147, but **Sarsabz by name
  in 15** and Fatima in 4. One farmer asks *"fawara khad kis company ki achi ha SAR sbz ya Fatima wali"* (which company's
  is better, Sarsabz or the Fatima one?). He doesn't know they're the same company.
- **Fatima's field practice and the creators' advice conflict, and farmers notice.** *"Fatima fertilizer walon ne NP+CAN+ZINC
  SULFATE mila ke chatta kiya hai result bhe behar hai aor aap bol rahai in ko aapus main na milaain"* (Fatima's people broadcast
  NP + CAN + zinc sulphate mixed and the result was better, and you say don't mix them). And: *"Ajeeb baat ha manufacturer kehty
  k can gawara last nitrogen doze for crop aur hmary experts 1st irrigation pr best sabit krny me lagy hoye, Sarsabz ka
  literature check kr"* (strange: the manufacturer says CAN is the last nitrogen dose, our experts push first irrigation;
  check the Sarsabz literature).
- **Some farmers here already report 55-70 maunds of wheat on DAP + urea.** Two give their inputs: (translated) "one bag DAP,
  three bags urea… 55/70 maunds", and *"Gandum ke Average 63-68 mann hoti hai. 1.DAP 1 bag 2.Urea 2 bag"*. Others report 33-50.
  That's thin, but it's enough to say a Ki Jeet winner's 69.9 maunds impresses this audience less than "double the national
  average" suggests. To them the relevant proof is a field like theirs, side by side.

## 2. What farmers are trying to solve

They're building a season's fertilizer plan, input by input, and asking strangers to sign it off. The typical comment is
a full recipe followed by a request for approval:

> *"Waqt kasht: 1 bag dap + enrich First pani: 1 bag nitrophas+ 1 bag zabardat urea Second pani: 1 bag nitrophas plus 1 bag
> can gawara+3kg sulphur Third pani: 25 kg soluble potash+ 20kg can gawara... Ye fertilizer plan kaisa ha? Area: district
> muzaffargarh"* (how is this fertilizer plan?)

The job isn't "choose a fertilizer brand". It's **"get the combination, timing and method right so nothing I paid for is wasted."**
Timing and dose (163 comments) and flood-versus-broadcast (149) follow mixing (220) for that reason.

## 3. Pain points

| Pain | What it means for Sarsabz |
|---|---|
| Fear that NP locks up when combined with zinc urea, sulphur or potash | The biggest practical barrier to NP in the file. It's a *usage* barrier, not a belief barrier |
| Contradictory expert advice, including against Fatima's own field practice | The brand's agronomy isn't reaching farmers as the authority; creators fill the gap |
| No settled answer to "how many NP bags replace one DAP?" | The switching argument is being made by third parties, inconsistently |
| Soil-specific doubt (sandy, saline, hard, rain-fed) | Claims made nationally; decisions made per soil type |
| Unanswered comments; requests for WhatsApp numbers | Demand for one-to-one advice exceeds what creators can give |
| Counterfeit DAP: *"DAP 2number boohat hai markit mein"* | Thin (5 comments), but it points to an authenticity advantage for a domestic, stickered product |

## 4. What farmers are looking for

| They seek | What it means for Sarsabz |
|---|---|
| A compatibility answer: what mixes with what | A simple, authoritative "mix / don't mix" guide for NP and CAN, signed by the maker |
| A per-acre, per-irrigation plan for *their* soil and district | Soil- and region-specific advice, not a national claim |
| Evidence, not opinion: (translated) "is there any written proof?" | Farmers ask for proof unprompted. Sarsabz holds 500+ demo plots and shows none |
| Someone who replies | The Sarsabz Pakistan App's advisory function has an audience waiting |

## 5. What they dislike

- **One rule this week, another next week.** *"app ne old videos me bataya hy k pehly pani pher can gawara dy do aur neche
  DAP khaad hota hy ,ab keh rhy ho k in dono ko mix na karo"* (in old videos you said give CAN on first irrigation with DAP
  below; now you say don't mix them).
- **Suspected paid advice.** *"Medicine mafia se paise lekar video banner rehta"* (takes money from the pesticide mafia to make
  videos); *"Mop sale kerne hay AP ko comny kitnay day ge"* (how much will the company pay you to sell MOP?). **Brand-sponsored
  creator content starts with a credibility deficit here.**
- **English and jargon.** *"Sir English mn to samjh hi nh ati"* (I don't understand the English); (translated) "use simple words,
  speak less English".
- **Cost without explanation.** (Translated) "CAN is a very expensive source of nitrogen" got the reply *"100% agreed"*.

## 7. Brands and products in the conversation

| Named | Comments | Read |
|---|---:|---|
| Urea (generic) | 230 | The default nitrogen; every plan starts from it |
| CAN / "gawara" | 193 | Recommended by creators for saline and sandy soils; seen as costly per unit of N |
| NP / Nitrophos | 147 | Questions are about *how*, almost never *whether* |
| DAP | 136 | The phosphate default; counterfeit worry attaches to it |
| **Engro Zabardast urea** | **89** | **The only fertilizer *brand* farmers name routinely**, and NP's most common companion |
| Other brands (Badshah SSP, Swat Agro, Jaffar SOP, Syngenta, FMC) | 26 | Specialty inputs |
| FFC Sona (urea, boron, zinc, SOP, new NPS) | 20 | Present, rarely argued about |
| **Sarsabz** | **15** | Mostly as "Sarsabz Nitrophos" inside someone else's recipe |
| Fatima | 4 | Including the "Sarsabz or Fatima?" confusion |
| Pak Arab | 2 | *"Can gawara or pak Arb MN Kya farq hai?"* (what's the difference between CAN gawara and Pak Arab?) |

Engro has done what Sarsabz hasn't: **Zabardast is a brand farmers ask for by name and reason about** (*"Zabardast Urea me
mojood zinc bio active form me hoti hai"*: the zinc in Zabardast is bio-active). Sarsabz makes the category's specialist products
and isn't the name attached to them.

## 8. Sarsabz, directly

**15 comments name Sarsabz. That's too few for a defensible SWOT.** What they show:

- The name travels as a qualifier inside recipes (*"Sarsabz Nitrophos+ Sulphur + Simple urea use krein"*), usually written by a
  creator or a helpful stranger, not a farmer describing the brand.
- The one head-to-head question (*"Daer ye pak arab k best hy ya sarsabz ki?"*: is Pak Arab's best, or Sarsabz's?) gets the
  answer *"Dono he behtreen hn"* (both are excellent). The group's two CAN brands compete for attention and nobody explains why both exist.
- The most consequential comment isn't one of the 15: **Fatima's own field team is described demonstrating an NP + CAN + zinc
  mix** that creators tell farmers is wrong (two near-identical comments, possibly the same person). The brand has agronomic
  authority on the ground and isn't using it on screen.

## 9. Strategic implications

| Direction | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Answer the mixing question, publicly and simply** | 220 comments; the fear is waste, and the answer is the maker's to give |
| **Make the switch arithmetic explicit** (bags of NP per bag of DAP, cost per acre, yield) | Farmers ask it; replies are inconsistent; the price gap favours NP |
| **Proof on the farmer's terms: side-by-side fields, named soil, named district** | Some already report 55-70 maunds; a national average impresses the category, not them |
| **Put the Sarsabz name on the products farmers already discuss** | 193 CAN and 147 NP mentions against 15 for Sarsabz; Engro shows it can be done |
| **Use creators carefully** | Farmers suspect paid advice; sponsored content needs visible evidence to survive |
| **Route the demand for replies to the Sarsabz Pakistan App** | Farmers ask for WhatsApp numbers; the app has 800K+ downloads and almost no on-screen presence |

## 10. Questions for the client

- Which videos did these comments come from (channels, dates)? Were any sponsored by Fatima?
- What is Fatima's official position on mixing NP with zinc-coated urea, sulphur and potash, and on CAN timing?
- Is the anti-counterfeit sticker on Nitrophos the same 9-digit code used for the lucky draw? Can a farmer use it to verify authenticity?
- Why do Sarsabz CAN and Pak Arab CAN both go to market, and how should a farmer choose?

## 11. Data and method

- **Base:** 1,433 comments after removing 82 interface-scaffolding cells and 15 too short to code (`load_report.md`).
  190 rows repeat text across 42 distinct comments; repeats aren't treated as corroboration.
- **Coding:** deterministic lexicon (`lexicon.yaml`), 17 themes and 11 brand/product groups, written after reading the whole file,
  with Roman Urdu and Urdu-script variants. One false-positive source was found and removed: the English word "can" matching CAN.
- **Coverage:** 54.1% of comments match at least one theme.
- **Limits:** self-selected viewers of agronomy channels, likely skewed to engaged, irrigated-Punjab wheat growers and heavy on
  the winter season. It can't support a share, a satisfaction rate or a claim about "most farmers". Quotes are what commenters
  wrote, not verified facts. Sentiment counts are directional only and aren't used above.
