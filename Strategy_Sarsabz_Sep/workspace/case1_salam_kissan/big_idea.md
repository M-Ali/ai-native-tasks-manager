# Salam Kissan 2026: The Big Idea (Case 1)

Method: `big-idea` skill. Three single-line insights from three bodies of evidence, combined into one organising thought.
Compiled 16 September 2026 under the rule **no made-up data, no assumptions**. Each insight is our reading of the evidence
listed under it. Nothing below is a measured fact unless a source is given. The brand insight is **provisional**.

---

## CONSUMER INSIGHT: relevance

**The people most moved by Salam Kissan are farmers and farmers' children speaking with pride about their own family, yet in seven years of films the farmer has almost never been the one speaking.**

Evidence: 1,572 YouTube comments on the 18 largest Salam Kissan uploads, all read (`comments/comment_findings.md`), plus the archive.
- "I feel proud because my  father is farmer." (2019 anthem, 123 likes: the most-liked comment on that film)
- "I'm daughter of farmer. My grandfather was a farmer. And I'm proud of it." (2019)
- "We love kissan because my father is farmer  Wo mehnat krta hn or ham aj parh raha hn" (2023). Our translation of the Urdu: "he works hard and today we are studying".
- The tension, from the archive: a farmer speaks in **9 of 76** Salam Kissan uploads, while celebrities and politicians speak in 30 (`archive/archive_findings.md`).

Limits:
- The commenters are self-selected, on brand uploads.
- The self-descriptions are quoted as signal, not counted.
- The corpus can't say how many commenters are farmers, how old they are, or where they live.

Candidates considered:
- "Farmers are saluted once a year but want to be valued every day." It's supported by "کاش اس ملک میں کسان کی قدر ہوتی" (38 likes) and "We need to make this permanent not temporary." (87 likes). But complaints are 19 of 1,516 comments, too thin to lead.
- "People respond to the song, not the salute." There are 103 music comments. It's true, but it's about execution, so it's kept for media principles.

## CATEGORY INSIGHT: distinctiveness

**Kissan Day has become a December salute any brand can post, even under the name "Salam Kissan", and the one brand moving toward the farmer's own voice is Syngenta, with #MaiKisanHun.**

Evidence: a YouTube search of 7 queries and 254 videos, with 36 non-Sarsabz uploads read (`scan/scan_findings.md`).
- **Sarsabz share:** 43 of the 168 search results titled for the day or Salam Kissan.
- **Borrowed name:** JPL Pakistan (2020) and Rizq Foods (2025) title their videos "Salam Kissan" / "Salam Kisan".
- **Syngenta Pakistan** posted December films in two years:
  - 2023, tagged #MaiKisanHun (our translation: "I am a farmer")
  - 2024, titled "کسان کا عالمی دن" (our translation: "world farmers' day")

  The films weren't watched, so how far they give the farmer a voice is unverified.
- **The critic:** "18 december kisan day sirf tv show per  kisan Haqeeqat main rull gia pakistan ka kisan" (DIGITAL KISAN Pakistan, 2022). Our translation: "Kissan Day is only on TV shows; in reality Pakistan's farmer is ruined".
- **FFC and Engro:** no Kissan Day campaign found on YouTube or in web search. TikTok and Facebook weren't covered.

## BRAND INSIGHT: ownership · PROVISIONAL

**Sarsabz gave Pakistan its Kissan Day, but the day now travels without the brand: it's reported under "Fatima Group" or no name at all, while Sarsabz's own output lives in one month and has thinned since 2023.**

Evidence:
- **The brief (p12):** Salam Kissan began in 2019, and the 2026 ask includes "strengthening our ownership of the cause".
- **The Nation, 19 Dec 2024:**
  - The 5th National Farmers' Day was observed by the Kashtkar Dost Foundation and Kissan Ittehad.
  - It credits "Fatima Group" for proposing the day.
  - Neither Sarsabz nor Salam Kissan is mentioned.
- **The archive:**
  - 60 of 76 uploads (79%) fall between 1 Dec and 15 Jan.
  - 2024 had no anthem-length film, only two 15-second farmer salutes and an event recap.
  - In 2025, five uploads were 3D/AI-animated farmers.
- **The comments:** the eight 2025 uploads drew 61 audience comments between them, 35 of them from four accounts.

It's provisional for two reasons. The brief's awards and TikTok results (p9-10) can't be checked without logging in. And no brand-health data on Salam Kissan awareness or attribution was supplied.

## CHANNEL INSIGHT

**The response comes on the long anthem films and when creators send their audiences, not on shorts.**

- The 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2023 anthems drew 1,422 of the 1,572 comments.
- In 2023, 24 commenters said they came from the Ducky Bhai or Shehr Main Dehat vlogs (names as commenters spell them vary).
- The eight 2025 uploads drew 61.
- Views aren't used, because brand-channel views are mostly paid.

## THE BIG IDEA

**Salam Kissan gets its reply: for the first time, the farmer answers Pakistan's salute in his own voice, through his own family.**

Pakistan has saluted the farmer for seven years. In 2026 the salute becomes a conversation. Farmers and their children answer: what the
work is, what it costs, what it gives the country. Sarsabz started the salute, and now it hands over the microphone.

**STRATEGY:** Re-frame Salam Kissan from a December tribute *about* farmers into a year-round conversation *with* them. Make farmers and
their children the voice of every piece, and carry their answers from sowing to harvest to 18 December.

**COMMS PLATFORM:** Position Sarsabz as the champion who gave the farmer his day, and now gives him his voice.

**Why it holds**
- **Relevant:** it starts from what commenters take pride in, family and the farmer's own work, not from borrowed voices.
- **Distinctive:** it moves off the generic salute that JPL, Rizq Foods and Syngenta already post.
- **Ownable:** only the brand that started the salute can credibly hand the reply back. #MaiKisanHun is the nearest threat, so Sarsabz has to move first and stay consistent.

**Remove-one test**
- **Without the consumer insight,** this is just a format change.
- **Without the category insight,** there's no reason to move away from an anthem that works.
- **Without the brand insight,** any brand could run "farmer voices".

**What it asks of Fatima**
1. **Real farmers and families on screen**, not celebrities or AI-animated farmers.
2. **Farmers' answers on air, complaints included.** They'll say what the comments already say: fertilizer supply, price, respect. Editing that out kills the idea and proves the critic ("sirf tv show") right.
3. **The brand name kept with the day** in all PR and event materials (Sarsabz, Salam Kissan), not only "Fatima Group". Whether "Salam Kissan" is a registered mark is a question for Fatima's legal team; we haven't assumed it.
4. **A campaign that runs beyond December**, across the Rabi (Oct-Mar) and Kharif (Apr-Sep) seasons named in the brief (p6).
5. **Organic response reported separately** from giveaway-driven response (the 2023 lesson).

**Media principles** (from the channel insight)
1. Keep a long anthem-scale film at the centre, because that's where the response is.
2. Use creators to carry the story to people who don't farm. 2023 showed referral traffic; whether it was paid is a question for the client.
3. Answer farmers in the comments, in their own language, and don't count contest entries as engagement.
4. Measure it with a brand-health question on who Pakistan credits for Kissan Day. The client would need to add it.

**Alternatives considered**
- **"A year of the farmer"** (a crop-calendar platform). It answers the "beyond a single day" ask (brief p12), but it carries no consumer tension, and any agri brand could run it.
- **"Farmers' children as the bridge to urban youth."** It suits the young audience the brief names, but the corpus can't show commenters' age or location, so it rests on an assumption. It's folded into the recommended idea as the voice, not as a claim about reach.
